What to do now.

A good daily session, 45–60 minutes

  1. Sounds (5–10 min). Ear training first, especially in your first month. Hear the difference before you try to say it.
  2. Review (15–20 min). Clear your due cards before anything new. Short and daily beats long and rare.
  3. New cards (10 min). Add a unit’s words, give each a picture or a sentence that is about your life.
  4. One lesson (15–20 min). Learn, read, then write and check.
  5. Speak (5–10 min). Say the speaking task aloud, record yourself, or use it with a tutor.
How this course works

Hear before you speak. Swedish vowel length and its front rounded vowels are the sounds that cost most learners their accent. The Sounds section trains your ear first.

Meaning without translation habits. Review cards show a meaning or a sentence with a gap, and you produce the Swedish. Gender is colour-coded and always shown with the noun: en blue, ett red.

Make it yours. A word sticks when it connects to your life. Each card takes a personal sentence and your own picture.

Spaced review. Cards come back just before you would forget them, so reviews stay short.

Grammar from sentences. Each unit teaches one pattern, then you meet it in a story, in cloze cards and in the grammar reference.

Conversation early. Learn the utility phrases first (Speak section) so you can run a session in Swedish from week one.

Pronunciation

Sounds first.

Swedish rewards a trained ear. Spend ten minutes here daily for the first month, then occasionally. Use the recordings you own as the real model: the device voice below is a guide and can be wrong on stress and melody.

1. Vowel length

In a stressed syllable, the vowel is either long (followed by one consonant) or short (followed by a long consonant, written double or as a cluster). Length is meaning: mat is food, matt is dull.

2. Rounded front vowels: i – u – y, e – ö

Swedish y is like i with rounded lips. Swedish u (hus) is a different rounded sound from o (bo), which is the “oo” vowel. Do not replace u with English “oo”. Watch lips in a mirror: y and ö are rounded, i and e are spread.

4. Word melody (accent 1 and 2)

Swedish words have one of two pitch patterns. Two words can differ only in this. Learn it from recordings, not from rules: listen to your CD tracks and copy whole phrases. Examples of pairs: anden (the duck) and anden (the spirit); buren (the cage) and buren (carried). The device voice often cannot show the difference.

5. Ear trainer

Listen, then choose which word you heard. Replay as often as you like. Your score is saved.

6. Record, listen, compare

Say a word or sentence, then compare with the recording or the device voice. Stop when it is clearly the same length and the same lip shape.

Conversation

Session plan with a tutor or partner

  1. Three minutes of warm-up: describe your day in Swedish.
  2. The unit’s speaking task, without reading it.
  3. Word-explaining round (below).
  4. Correction round: the tutor notes three errors, not thirty.
  5. Afterwards, turn each corrected sentence into a card (Review, Add my own card).

Explain it without the word

When you do not know a word, describe it. Practise with these starters: Det är ett slags …, Det är motsatsen till …, Man använder det för att …, Det finns i …. Pick a random word from your due cards and explain it in Swedish in thirty seconds.

Four-pass listening routine

  1. Listen once without pausing. What situation can you recognise?
  2. Listen again. Write three words and any names, numbers or times you hear.
  3. Loop a short passage. Copy its rhythm aloud, then say it again without playback.
  4. Write one sentence summarising the passage. Check it with your adult partner.
